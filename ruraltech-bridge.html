Project Overview

Founder and Project Lead | Ghana | Educational Technology | Ongoing Initiative

Early Product Development

RuralTech Bridge is an educational technology initiative I founded to explore practical approaches to improving technology education and digital inclusion in rural and underserved communities in Ghana.

The initiative focuses on an important challenge: students are expected to develop computing and digital skills, yet some schools have limited access to computers, reliable electricity, internet connectivity, and other technology infrastructure.

Rather than waiting for every school to have a fully equipped computer laboratory, RuralTech Bridge explores learning experiences that introduce foundational computing concepts using affordable, accessible, and locally appropriate educational materials.

Project Goal

Create an affordable, hands-on learning kit that supports computing education in schools with limited access to digital devices.

The Problem

Computer and digital skills are increasingly important for education, employment, communication, and participation in a technology-driven society.

However, teaching computing can be challenging in schools where students have limited opportunities to interact directly with computers and where electricity or internet connectivity may be unreliable or unavailable.

This creates an educational access gap: students may be expected to learn about computers and digital technologies without having consistent access to the technologies they are studying.

Limited Computer Access

Students may have few opportunities to practice with physical computers during lessons.

Infrastructure Challenges

Unreliable electricity and internet connectivity can make technology-based teaching difficult.

Teaching Materials

Schools may lack affordable materials for demonstrating computing concepts through practical activities.

Learning Opportunities

Limited resources can reduce students' opportunities for interactive and collaborative learning.

Current Product Concept

Computer Science Without Computers

The current RuralTech Bridge product concept is Computer Science Without Computers , an offline learning kit designed to help students learn foundational computing concepts through hands-on activities without requiring continuous access to a computer.

The approach translates computing concepts into physical, collaborative, and classroom-friendly activities that can be used in low-resource learning environments.

Design Principle

The goal is not to replace access to computers. Instead, the learning kit is intended to provide a practical bridge that allows students to begin developing computing knowledge while schools continue working toward greater access to digital infrastructure.

Learning Kit Materials

The developing learning-kit concept includes physical and teacher-supported materials such as:

Picture Cards

Cards representing computer components and digital technologies.

Name Cards

Cards that help students identify computing devices and components.

Function Cards

Materials explaining what different computer components do.

Matching Activities

Activities connecting computer components with their names and functions.

Challenge Cards

Activities encouraging students to apply their understanding through classroom tasks.

Learning Mats

Materials for organizing collaborative and hands-on activities.

Teacher-Support Materials

Guidance explaining how teachers can facilitate activities using the learning kit.

These materials describe the current product concept and development work. The complete kit has not yet undergone school-based evaluation.

How the Product Is Intended to Work

Students interact with physical learning materials to identify, classify, match, organize, and explain computing concepts.

Activities are designed to encourage participation and discussion rather than relying only on textbook descriptions.

Example Learning Activity

Students could match an image of a computer component with its name and function, organize components according to their roles, and complete challenges requiring them to explain how different parts of a computer system work together.

This approach is intended to make abstract computing concepts more tangible while supporting collaborative learning in classrooms where access to digital devices may be limited.

Needs Assessment

Early development of the project included an initial needs survey with 13 respondents to better understand technology access, teaching challenges, and opportunities for supporting computing education in resource-constrained schools.

The needs-assessment process helped refine the problem definition, target users, value proposition, and direction of the initial learning-kit concept.

Research Contribution

The needs survey provided early contextual information for product development. It was not a usability evaluation of the finished kit.

The survey was an exploratory activity involving a small number of respondents. Its purpose was to inform early decisions rather than establish findings representative of all rural schools in Ghana.

Human-Centered Design Approach

RuralTech Bridge applies a human-centered approach by beginning with the needs, constraints, and learning environments of the people who will use the product.

Design Consideration Product Direction
Infrastructure Activities that can be used without continuous electricity, internet access, or computers.
Affordability Explore low-cost materials suitable for resource-constrained classrooms.
Teacher Facilitation Include guidance for organizing and delivering learning activities.
Student Participation Encourage hands-on activities, discussion, and collaboration.
Classroom Usability Consider how teachers and students will interact with the materials during lessons.
Local Context Consider the realities of rural and underserved learning environments.

The project is being developed iteratively so that feedback from educators, students, mentors, and other stakeholders can inform future versions.

Product Development Process

1. Problem Identification

Identified the challenge of teaching computing concepts in schools with limited access to computers, electricity, and internet connectivity.

2. Needs Assessment

Gathered initial feedback through a survey with 13 respondents to better understand technology access, classroom challenges, and potential user needs.

3. Product Concept

Developed the Computer Science Without Computers concept as an offline, hands-on approach to foundational computing education.

4. Prototype Development

Began developing learning cards, activity materials, classroom challenges, learning mats, and teacher-support resources.

5. Future Testing

Future work will include prototype evaluation with educators and students, refinement of learning activities, and school-based pilot testing.

Current Development Stage

Prototype Development and Refinement

RuralTech Bridge is currently in the early product-development and prototype-refinement stage.

The immediate focus is on communicating the product concept clearly, strengthening the structure of the learning kit, developing additional prototype materials, and preparing for future testing with intended users.

The current work represents an evolving educational technology prototype, not a fully deployed school program.

Future Development

The next phase of the project is intended to focus on refining the product and evaluating its suitability for classroom use.

  • Expand the range of Computer Science Without Computers learning activities.
  • Develop additional physical prototypes and teacher-support resources.

  • Test learning activities with students in appropriate educational settings.
  • Refine the product based on educator and student feedback.
  • Explore partnerships with schools, educational organizations, and community stakeholders.
  • Conduct pilot implementations to evaluate feasibility, usability, and educational value.
Research Status

Project Impact and Long-Term Goal

RuralTech Bridge explores how educational technology can be designed around the realities of communities where access to digital infrastructure remains limited.

Computer Science Without Computers aims to reduce the barrier between students and foundational computing education by creating learning experiences that can begin with the resources already available in the classroom.

The longer-term goal is to contribute to more inclusive pathways into computing and digital learning for students in rural and underserved communities.

The educational impact of the learning kit will need to be evaluated through future classroom implementation and research.
