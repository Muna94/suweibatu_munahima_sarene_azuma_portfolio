Project Overview

This project analyzed 22,437 student records from the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset (OULAD) to investigate whether early engagement behaviors can predict academic success in online learning environments.

Using logistic regression and learning analytics techniques, I examined how Early Clicks and Early Active Days relate to the likelihood of students successfully completing a course.

The project demonstrates the application of educational data mining and predictive analytics to identify indicators of student success.

Research Question

Can early engagement behaviors predict whether a student will successfully pass a course?

The analysis examined students' interactions with online learning materials during the first 21 days of a course.

Two engagement indicators were considered: the number of early clicks and the number of days students were active in the learning environment.

Dataset

The study used the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset (OULAD) , which contains information about students' online learning activities and academic outcomes.

The analysis included 22,437 student records.

Key Findings

  • Students who passed demonstrated higher early engagement. The analysis showed differences in early engagement between students who passed and those who failed.
  • Early Clicks emerged as the strongest predictor. Students with more interactions with learning materials during the first 21 days were more likely to pass.
  • Early Active Days also contributed to prediction. The findings suggest that engagement consistency, alongside engagement intensity, is relevant to academic outcomes.
  • Predicted passing probability increased with engagement. The logistic regression visualizations showed higher predicted probabilities of passing at higher levels of early engagement.
  • Early engagement may help identify students who need support. Engagement indicators could provide useful information for educators seeking to recognize potential academic difficulties earlier.

Implications for Education

The findings highlight the potential value of examining early student engagement when considering academic support.

Information about student activity could help educators recognize engagement patterns and decide when additional follow-up may be appropriate.

However, online engagement measures do not fully capture student understanding, motivation, or personal circumstances.

Predictive information should therefore support educators' decisions rather than replace professional judgment.

This project examined predictive relationships. It did not evaluate an implemented student intervention.
