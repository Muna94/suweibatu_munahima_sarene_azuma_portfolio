Project Overview

As a Graduate Research Assistant in the Center for Human, Artificial Intelligence, and Robot Teaming (CHART) at Arizona State University, I contribute to a human-subjects research study evaluating the effectiveness and participant experience of the NIRSense neural stimulation device.

The study uses a controlled human-autonomy teaming environment to investigate human performance, team coordination, workload, and participant experience during collaborative tasks involving human and autonomous team members.

My work spans study preparation, participant-session support, experimental protocol implementation, behavioral data collection, data organization and quality review, usability evaluation, and research documentation.

Research Problem

Human-autonomy teams are increasingly used in aviation, robotics, healthcare, and other complex work environments. Understanding how people coordinate, communicate, and perform alongside advanced technologies is important for designing safe, effective, and human-centered systems.

This project applies human factors methods to evaluate team performance, workload, coordination, and participant experience during laboratory-based collaborative tasks.

Human Factors Evaluation

A central focus of my work is understanding the human experience within the experimental system. In addition to task-performance data, the study considers workload, perceived coordination, participant comfort, usability, and experiences interacting with the experimental technology.

These measures help connect system performance with the experience of the people using the technology, supporting a human-centered approach to the evaluation of emerging neurotechnology and autonomous systems.

Research Impact

This project contributes to research examining the relationship between neurotechnology, human performance, workload, coordination, and participant experience in complex team environments.

By combining controlled experimentation, behavioral measures, participant experience measures, and human-autonomy teaming tasks, the research contributes to understanding how emerging technologies can be evaluated from both performance and human-centered perspectives.

The broader goal is to inform the design and evaluation of safer, more effective, and more human-centered technologies for collaborative work.
