Influence of AI Communication Valence on Communication Sentiment and Collaborative Task Performance in Human-AI Teams

Project Type: Master's Thesis | Human-AI Interaction | Human Factors | Quantitative Research

Ongoing Master's thesis research investigating whether positive versus negative emotional markers in AI teammate communication influence human communication sentiment and collaborative task performance in human-AI teams.

Study Context: The research uses data from a controlled human-AI teaming experiment involving 72 participants organized into 36 teams. Each team consisted of two human participants working with a Wizard-of-Oz AI teammate on a collaborative task.

Research Approach: Human communication sentiment is quantified using VADER sentiment analysis and examined alongside team-performance measures, including task accuracy and performance efficiency.

Analysis: Independent-samples t-tests, Pearson correlations, and hierarchical multiple regression are used to examine communication sentiment and performance relationships.

Tools: SPSS, Python, VADER, and Excel.

Skills Demonstrated: Human-AI interaction, experimental research, sentiment analysis, quantitative research, statistical analysis, human factors, and collaborative-team research.

Critical Code Reviewer (CCR) - UX Research and Responsible AI

Project Type: UX Research | Usability Research | Human-Centered AI | Responsible AI

Contributed to the development of the Critical Code Reviewer (CCR) Framework and subsequently designed user-centered research procedures to evaluate both the CCR Framework and the supporting CCR Tool.

Spring 2026 - Framework Development: Conducted literature reviews and research synthesis and contributed to the development of the original framework dimensions, evaluation criteria, definitions, and developer-facing indicators.

The original framework addressed seven dimensions: Fairness Evaluation, Pedagogy, Decision Fairness, Inclusion, Transparency, Privacy, and AI Governance.

Summer 2026 - UX Research Study Design: Designed and refined procedures and research instruments for a two-phase sequential qualitative study involving educational technology stakeholders and software developers.

Research Methods: Focus groups, critical-incident prompts, framework-reaction activities, independent and CCR-supported code-review tasks, concurrent think-aloud protocols, hybrid card sorting using FigJam, retrospective semi-structured interviews, and qualitative Template Analysis.

Study Focus: Evaluating the clarity, relevance, organization, practicality, and usability of CCR Framework v5.7, alongside developers' experiences using the CCR Tool.

Research Status: Research design and study preparation. Participant evaluation findings are not presented as completed results.

Skills Demonstrated: UX research, usability evaluation planning, qualitative research design, research protocol development, focus group design, think-aloud protocols, card sorting, interview design, research synthesis, and responsible AI evaluation.

Predicting Student Success from Early Engagement Behaviors

Project Type: Educational Data Analytics | Predictive Modeling | Quantitative Research

Analyzed 22,437 student records from the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset to investigate whether early engagement behaviors can predict course success.

Developed logistic regression models using early clicks and active learning days, created predictive probability visualizations, and identified indicators associated with academic performance.

Dataset: Open University Learning Analytics Dataset, 22,437 student records.

Methods: Data cleaning, feature engineering, exploratory data analysis, logistic regression, odds ratio analysis, and predictive modeling.

Tools: R, ggplot2, and data visualization.

Skills Demonstrated: Predictive analytics, educational data mining, statistical modeling, quantitative research, data visualization, and model interpretation.

RuralTech Bridge — Computer Science Without Computers

Project Type: Human-Centered Design | Educational Technology | Digital Inclusion

Founded RuralTech Bridge, an educational technology initiative developing practical approaches to technology education for rural and underserved communities in Ghana.

Current Product: Developing Computer Science Without Computers, an offline learning kit designed to help upper-primary students learn foundational computing concepts in schools with limited access to computers, electricity, and internet connectivity.

Product Development: The concept includes computer-component cards, picture-and-name matching activities, function cards, challenge activities, learning mats, and teacher-support materials.

Human-Centered Research: Conducted an initial needs survey with 13 respondents and used feedback from educators and venture-development activities to refine the problem definition, target users, value proposition, and prototype direction.

Current Stage: Early-stage product development and prototype refinement, with future plans to evaluate prototypes with educators and students and pilot the approach in schools.

Skills Demonstrated: Needs assessment, user-centered research, human-centered design, educational technology, product development, prototyping, digital inclusion, and community engagement.
