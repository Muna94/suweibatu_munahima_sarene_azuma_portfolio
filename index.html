Influence of AI Communication Valence on Communication Sentiment and Collaborative Task Performance in Human-AI Teams

Project Type: Master's Thesis | Human-AI Interaction | Human Factors

Ongoing Master's thesis research investigating whether positive versus negative emotional markers in AI teammate communication influence human communication sentiment and collaborative task performance in human-AI teams.

Study Context: The research uses data from a controlled human-AI teaming experiment involving 72 participants organized into 36 teams. Each team consisted of two human participants working with a Wizard-of-Oz AI teammate on a collaborative task.

Research Approach: Human communication sentiment is quantified using VADER sentiment analysis and examined alongside team-performance measures including task accuracy and performance efficiency.

Planned Analysis: Statistical analyses examine differences in human communication sentiment across AI communication-valence conditions, relationships between communication sentiment and performance, and whether human communication sentiment predicts performance while accounting for AI communication condition.

Tools: SPSS, P, VADER, and Excel.

Skills Demonstrated: Human-AI interaction, experimental research, sentiment analysis, quantitative research, statistical analysis, human factors, research design, and collaborative-team research.

Critical Code Reviewer (CCR) Framework

Project Type: Responsible AI | Human-Centered AI | Educational Technology

Contributed to the development and ongoing user-centered evaluation of the Critical Code Reviewer (CCR) Framework, a responsible AI approach designed to help educational technology developers identify ethical, fairness, accessibility, transparency, privacy, and governance concerns during software development and code review.

Framework Development: Conducted research synthesis and contributed to the development and refinement of framework dimensions, evaluation criteria, definitions, and developer-facing indicators.

Current Research: Contributing to the design of a two-phase sequential qualitative validation study involving educational technology stakeholders and software developers. The planned study uses focus groups, framework-reaction activities, think-aloud code review, hybrid card sorting, retrospective interviews, and qualitative Template Analysis.

Framework Refinement: Findings are designed to support iterative refinement from the literature-informed CCR Version 1.0 to stakeholder-informed Version 2.0 and developer-informed Version 3.0.

Skills Demonstrated: Responsible AI, Human-Computer Interaction, qualitative research, framework development, framework validation, educational technology, usability evaluation, and interdisciplinary collaboration.

Predicting Student Success from Early Engagement Behaviors

Project Type: Educational Data Analytics | Predictive Modeling

Analyzed 22,437 student records from the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset to investigate whether early engagement behaviors can predict course success.

Developed logistic regression models using early clicks and active learning days, created predictive probability visualizations, and identified key indicators associated with academic performance.

Dataset: Open University Learning Analytics Dataset, 22,437 student records.

Methods: Data cleaning, feature engineering, exploratory data analysis, logistic regression, odds ratio analysis, and predictive modeling.

Tools: R, ggplot2, statistical learning, and data visualization.

Skills Demonstrated: Predictive analytics, educational data mining, statistical modeling, research design, data visualization, and model interpretation.

RuralTech Bridge — Computer Science Without Computers

Project Type: Educational Technology | Digital Inclusion | Human-Centered Design

Founded RuralTech Bridge, an educational technology initiative developing practical approaches to technology education for rural and underserved communities in Ghana.

Current Product: Developing Computer Science Without Computers, an offline learning kit designed to help upper-primary students learn foundational computing concepts in schools with limited access to computers, electricity, and internet connectivity.

Product Development: The current concept uses hands-on learning materials including computer-component cards, picture-and-name matching activities, function cards, challenge activities, learning mats, and teacher-support materials.

Human-Centered Approach: Conducted an initial needs survey with 13 respondents and used feedback from educators and venture-development activities to refine the problem definition, target users, value proposition, and prototype direction.

Current Stage: Early-stage product development and prototype refinement, with future plans to test prototypes with educators and students and pilot the approach in schools.

Skills Demonstrated: Educational technology, human-centered design, needs assessment, product development, prototyping, digital inclusion, entrepreneurship, community engagement, and technology education.
