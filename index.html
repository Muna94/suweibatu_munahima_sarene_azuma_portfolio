Influence of AI Communication Valence on Communication Sentiment and Collaborative Task Performance in Human-AI Teams

Project Type: Master's Thesis | Human-AI Interaction | Human Factors | Quantitative Research

My Master's thesis examines whether positive and negative emotional markers in AI teammate communication influence human communication sentiment and collaborative task performance.

Study Context: The research uses data from a controlled experiment involving 72 participants organized into 36 teams. Each team included two human participants working with a Wizard-of-Oz AI teammate.

Research Approach: Human communication sentiment is measured using VADER sentiment analysis and examined alongside team performance measures, including task accuracy and performance efficiency.

Analysis: Independent-samples t-tests, Pearson correlations, and hierarchical multiple regression are used to examine communication sentiment and performance relationships.

Tools: SPSS, Python, VADER, and Excel.

Skills Demonstrated: Experimental research, quantitative research, sentiment analysis, statistical analysis, human-AI interaction, and human factors.

Project Status: Ongoing Master's thesis research.

Critical Code Reviewer (CCR): UX Research and Responsible AI

Project Type: UX Research | Usability Research | Human-Centered AI | Responsible AI

Contributed to the development of the Critical Code Reviewer Framework and later designed user-centered research procedures to evaluate both the framework and the supporting CCR Tool.

Spring 2026 Framework Development: Conducted literature reviews and research synthesis and contributed to the original framework dimensions, evaluation criteria, definitions, and developer-facing indicators.

The original framework addressed seven dimensions: Fairness Evaluation, Pedagogy, Decision Fairness, Inclusion, Transparency, Privacy, and AI Governance.

Summer 2026 UX Research Study Design: Designed and refined research procedures and instruments for a two-phase sequential qualitative study involving educational technology stakeholders and software developers.

Research Methods: Focus groups, critical-incident prompts, framework-reaction activities, independent and CCR-supported code-review tasks, think-aloud protocols, hybrid card sorting using FigJam, retrospective semi-structured interviews, and qualitative Template Analysis.

Study Focus: Evaluating the clarity, relevance, organization, practicality, and usability of CCR Framework v5.7 and developers' experiences with the CCR Tool.

Project Status: Research design and study preparation. Participant evaluation findings are not presented as completed results.

Skills Demonstrated: UX research, qualitative research design, usability evaluation planning, focus group design, think-aloud protocols, card sorting, interview design, research synthesis, and responsible AI evaluation.

Human-Centered Automation of a School Computing Laboratory

Project Type: Human Factors | Human-Automation Interaction | UX Design | Conceptual Dashboard Design

Developed a conceptual human-centered automation system to support teachers in managing school computing laboratories. The project draws on my previous teaching experience at Tamale Girls Senior High School in Ghana.

Problem: Teachers spend time preparing computers, checking equipment readiness, troubleshooting faults during lessons, and verifying computer shutdown after practical classes.

Research and Design Approach: Conducted task analysis of laboratory activities before, during, and after lessons. Developed a function-allocation approach to determine which responsibilities should be handled by teachers, students, and automation.

Figma Dashboard: Designed a conceptual teacher-facing dashboard displaying computer status, detected faults, unknown conditions, equipment alerts, and teacher controls.

Human Factors Principles: Applied function allocation, situation awareness, automation transparency, appropriate trust, and human supervisory control.

Scenario-Based Assessment: Examined four representative situations: normal laboratory preparation, equipment faults, inaccurate alerts, and automation failures.

Tools: Figma, literature review, task analysis, and scenario-based assessment.

Project Status: Conceptual design developed in Fall 2026. Functional implementation and usability testing are planned as future work.

Skills Demonstrated: Human factors, human-automation interaction, task analysis, function allocation, dashboard design, UX design, interface design, and scenario-based assessment.

Predicting Student Success from Early Engagement Behaviors

Project Type: Educational Data Analytics | Predictive Modeling | Quantitative Research

Analyzed 22,437 student records from the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset to investigate whether early engagement behaviors can predict course success.

Research Approach: Developed logistic regression models using early clicks and active learning days, created predictive probability visualizations, and examined indicators associated with academic performance.

Methods: Data cleaning, feature engineering, exploratory data analysis, logistic regression, odds ratio analysis, and predictive modeling.

Tools: R, ggplot2, and data visualization.

Skills Demonstrated: Quantitative research, predictive analytics, educational data mining, statistical modeling, data visualization, and model interpretation.

RuralTech Bridge: Computer Science Without Computers

Project Type: Human-Centered Design | Educational Technology | Digital Inclusion

Founded RuralTech Bridge, an educational technology initiative developing practical approaches to technology education for rural and underserved communities in Ghana.

Current Product: Developing Computer Science Without Computers, an offline learning kit designed to help upper-primary students learn foundational computing concepts in schools with limited access to computers, electricity, and internet connectivity.

Product Development: The concept includes computer-component cards, picture-and-name matching activities, function cards, challenge activities, learning mats, and teacher-support materials.

Human-Centered Research: Conducted an initial needs survey with 13 respondents and used feedback from educators and venture-development activities to refine the problem definition, target users, value proposition, and prototype direction.

Current Stage: Early-stage product development and prototype refinement, with future plans to evaluate prototypes with educators and students.

Skills Demonstrated: Needs assessment, user-centered research, human-centered design, educational technology, prototyping, digital inclusion, and community engagement.
