Project Overview

Master's Thesis | Arizona State University | Human Systems Engineering

Research Analysis

This research investigates how the emotional tone of an artificial intelligence teammate's communication relates to human communication sentiment and collaborative task performance.

The study focuses on human-AI teams completing a collaborative task with an AI teammate whose communication was designed to express either positive or negative emotional valence.

My master's thesis uses a secondary analysis of archived experimental data to examine whether AI communication valence influences human communication sentiment and how human sentiment relates to team performance.

72 Participants in Parent Study
36 Human-AI Teams
2 AI Valence Conditions
VADER Sentiment Analysis
Research Focus

Understanding whether the emotional tone of AI teammate communication is associated with changes in human communication sentiment and collaborative task performance.

Research Problem

AI systems are increasingly being designed to communicate and collaborate with people in team-based environments.

Beyond providing information, AI teammates may use communication styles that convey different emotional tones.

These communication choices raise important questions about how humans respond to AI teammates and whether communication sentiment is associated with collaborative performance.

This project examines those relationships using communication data and task-performance measures from a controlled experiment.

Research Questions

Research Question 1

Does AI communication valence influence human communication sentiment in human-AI teams?

Research Question 2

Is human communication sentiment associated with collaborative task performance?

Research Question 3

Does human communication sentiment explain additional variation in task performance after accounting for AI communication valence?

Study Design

Parent Experiment

The original study used a 2 × 2 mixed factorial design involving AI communication valence and feedback order.

AI communication valence had two conditions: positive and negative. Feedback order involved neutral-first and valenced-first sequences.

The experiment included 72 human participants organized into 36 teams. Each team included two human members and a Wizard-of-Oz AI teammate.

Thesis Analysis

My thesis focuses on the valenced portion of the experimental task rather than analyzing the complete mixed factorial design.

Important Distinction

The original experiment generated the archived data. My thesis examines selected communication and performance variables through secondary statistical analysis.

Team Roles

Controller

A human team member participating in the collaborative task. Controller communication was included in the sentiment analysis.

Guide

A human team member participating in the collaborative task. Guide communication was included in the sentiment analysis.

AI Teammate

A Wizard-of-Oz AI teammate whose communication expressed the assigned positive or negative valence.

Variables and Measures

AI Communication Valence Independent Variable Positive or negative AI communication condition.

Guide Sentiment Human Communication Measure Mean VADER compound sentiment score for Guide communication.
Team Human Sentiment Predictor Average of the Controller and Guide mean sentiment scores for each team.
Total Correct Steps Performance Outcome Number of correctly completed task steps, ranging from 0 to 10.
Performance Efficiency Performance Outcome Composite performance measure incorporating correct steps, time remaining, and hints used.

Research Methods and Analysis

1. Communication Data

The analysis uses transcribed communication from the collaborative task to examine human communication sentiment.

2. Sentiment Analysis

Applied the Valence Aware Dictionary and sEntiment Reasoner (VADER) to quantify communication sentiment using compound scores.

Sentiment scores were examined for the Controller and Guide roles and combined into a team-level human sentiment measure.

3. Independent-Samples t-Tests

Used independent-samples t-tests to examine whether human communication sentiment differed between positive and negative AI valence conditions.

4. Pearson Correlation

Examined relationships between communication sentiment and task-performance measures using two-tailed Pearson correlation analysis.

5. Hierarchical Multiple Regression

Used hierarchical multiple regression to examine whether team human sentiment explained additional variation in performance after accounting for AI communication valence.

Selected Results

The analyses examined whether the AI valence manipulation produced differences in human communication sentiment and whether sentiment was related to performance outcomes.

AI Valence Manipulation

The sentiment analysis indicated that AI messages differed between the positive and negative valence conditions.

The manipulation check showed a statistically significant difference in AI message sentiment, t(34) = -9.66, p < .001 .

Human Communication Sentiment

The independent-samples t-tests did not identify statistically significant differences in the examined human communication sentiment measures between the AI valence conditions.

Sentiment Measure t p
Controller Sentiment -0.28 .778
Guide Sentiment 0.14 .891
AI-Guide Chat Sentiment 0.76 .455

Performance Accuracy Regression

In a hierarchical regression model predicting performance accuracy, neither AI communication valence nor team human sentiment was a statistically significant predictor in the model containing both variables.

The coefficient for team human sentiment was B = 5.322, p = .536 . The coefficient for AI communication valence was B = -0.731, p = .294 .

Interpretation

These results show that the AI message valence manipulation was distinguishable in the communication data, but the examined human sentiment measures did not show significant differences between valence conditions.

The selected accuracy regression results also did not provide evidence that team human sentiment significantly predicted performance accuracy after accounting for AI communication valence.

These are selected results from the ongoing thesis analysis and should not be interpreted as the complete findings of the project.

Human Factors and Human-AI Interaction

This research contributes to understanding how communication characteristics of AI teammates relate to human behavior in collaborative tasks.

It also highlights the importance of distinguishing between successfully manipulating the communication style of an AI system and demonstrating measurable changes in human communication or team performance.

These questions are relevant to the design and evaluation of AI systems intended to collaborate with human users.
