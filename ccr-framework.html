Project Overview

Arizona State University | Learning Engineering Institute | Spring 2026 to Present

The Critical Code Reviewer (CCR) project explores how responsible AI principles can be incorporated into educational technology software development.

The project includes two related components: the CCR Framework and the CCR Tool.

The CCR Framework provides research-informed dimensions and evaluation criteria for identifying ethical, educational, accessibility, fairness, privacy, and governance concerns during code review.

The CCR Tool supports the application of responsible AI review guidance within software-development workflows.

My contributions span two research stages. During Spring 2026, I contributed to the literature-informed development of the original framework. During Summer 2026, I shifted to designing a user-centered research study to evaluate the framework and supporting tool.

My Research Focus: Framework development, qualitative UX research design, research instruments, and usability evaluation planning.

Research Problem

Educational technology systems increasingly use automated decisions, predictive models, and AI-generated content to support teaching and learning.

However, conventional software code reviews may not systematically address how software decisions affect learners, educators, accessibility, fairness, transparency, privacy, and accountability.

The CCR project responds to this problem by providing a structured framework for identifying responsible AI concerns and a supporting tool intended to assist developers in applying review guidance.

A further user research question is whether the framework is understandable and practical for its intended users, and how developers experience the supporting CCR Tool during code review.

Spring 2026: Framework Development

During my Spring 2026 internship at Arizona State University's Learning Engineering Institute, I contributed to the initial development of the CCR Framework.

This work involved reviewing literature on responsible AI, educational technology, fairness, pedagogy, inclusion, accessibility, transparency, privacy, and AI governance.

We translated findings from the literature into structured dimensions, evaluation criteria, definitions, and indicators intended to guide responsible educational technology code review.

My work focused on research synthesis and framework development. I did not develop the CCR Tool or personally lead the later revisions that produced Framework v5.7.

Original Framework Dimensions

The framework developed during my initial internship organized responsible AI concerns into seven dimensions.

Dimension Focus
Fairness Evaluation Representation in datasets, fairness evaluation attributes, and group-level fairness targets.
Pedagogy Educational appropriateness, adaptive learning, learner progression, and instructional decisions.
Decision Fairness Fairness in decision rules, protected attributes, proxy variables, and mitigation controls.
Inclusion Accessibility, language, cultural representation, and equitable access.
Transparency Explainability, clarity of automated decisions, and opportunities for human review.
Privacy Consent, responsible use, and protection of learner information.
AI Governance Accountability, monitoring, review processes, and responsible deployment practices.

Examples of Framework Evaluation Criteria

The framework translated broad responsible AI principles into more specific concerns that developers could examine during software review.

  • Unrepresentative Dataset Coverage
  • Undefined Fairness Evaluation Attributes
  • Missing Group-Wise Fairness Targets
  • Unvalidated Early-Warning Thresholds
  • Hidden Learning Path Requirements
  • Protected and Proxy-Based Differential Treatment
  • Culturally Narrow Representations and Validation
  • Exclusionary Language in Code and UI
  • Opaque AI Decision Outputs
  • Missing Consent Checks for Learner Data
  • Missing Post-Deployment Risk Monitoring

These examples illustrate the literature-informed framework structure developed during the initial research phase. They are not presented as the complete criteria list for CCR Framework v5.7.

Summer 2026: UX Research and Usability Study Design

Following my framework-development internship, my work shifted toward designing a user-centered research study to evaluate both the CCR Framework and the CCR Tool.

The study is designed to examine how educational technology stakeholders interpret the framework's ethical concerns and how software developers understand and apply the framework and supporting tool during code-review activities.

The research uses a two-phase sequential qualitative design.

The framework version selected for the study is CCR Framework v5.7. This is distinct from the earlier framework version I helped develop during Spring 2026.

Research Objectives

  • Examine whether the CCR Framework represents ethical and educational technology concerns that stakeholders consider important.
  • Investigate how intended users understand the framework's dimensions, criteria, and terminology.
  • Explore how developers organize and interpret framework criteria.
  • Examine developers' experiences using the CCR Tool during code-review tasks.
  • Identify areas where framework clarity, organization, practicality, and usability could be improved.
  • Use qualitative feedback to inform future framework and tool refinement.

Research Design

Study Phase Participants Research Focus Methods
Phase 1: Stakeholder Evaluation Educational technology stakeholders Ethical concerns, framework coverage, clarity, and relevance Focus groups, critical-incident prompts, framework-reaction activities, and qualitative analysis
Phase 2: Developer Evaluation Software developers Framework interpretation, code-review practices, tool experience, and usability Code-review tasks, think-aloud protocols, hybrid card sorting, retrospective interviews, and qualitative analysis

Phase 1: Educational Technology Stakeholder Evaluation

The first phase is designed to explore ethical and educational concerns encountered by people involved in educational technology.

Participants are invited to discuss experiences and examine how well the CCR Framework represents concerns relevant to their work.

Planned Research Activities

  • Participant demographic questionnaire
  • Focus group discussions
  • Critical-incident discussion prompts
  • Framework-reaction activities
  • Plain-language scenarios
  • Researcher observations and field notes
  • Qualitative Template Analysis

This phase is intended to identify gaps, unclear concepts, and opportunities for framework refinement.

Phase 2: Software Developer Evaluation

The second phase is designed to investigate how software developers review educational technology code and how their experience changes when they use the CCR Framework and Tool.

Developers complete an independent code-review activity followed by a CCR-supported review.

The research procedures are intended to capture how developers interpret review guidance, identify ethical concerns, and experience the supporting CCR Tool.

Planned Research Activities

  • Developer demographic questionnaire
  • Independent code-review task
  • CCR-supported code-review task
  • Concurrent think-aloud protocol
  • Hybrid card sorting using FigJam
  • Retrospective semi-structured interviews
  • Researcher observations and field notes
  • Qualitative Template Analysis

UX Research Methods and Their Purpose
